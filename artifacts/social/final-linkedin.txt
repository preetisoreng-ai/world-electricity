The current beneath the crossover. In 2025 clean power grew faster than world demand, and renewables, led by hydro, edged past coal for the first time.

I turned Ember's world electricity data (2000–2025) into a data story; the opening chart moves like the skeleton of a transparent fish. The 43-second version:
• 13,507 TWh clean generation in 2025, against 18,418 TWh fossil
• 33.5% / 33.2%: renewables and coal, as shares of world electricity in 2025
• +879 TWh clean generation added in 2025, against 855 TWh of total growth
• 2,807 TWh solar generation, passing wind’s 2,705 TWh
• 0.3 points: renewables’ lead over coal. A 0.44% revision or a dry hydro year would undo it. Without hydro, renewables are 19.6%. One year is not a trend.

About this project
The world electricity series is an independent data-visualisation project built entirely from public data: Ember's yearly electricity generation data, World, all sources, 2000 to 2025. Every number can be reproduced from the published source.

How it was made
I built it with my own approval-gated AI framework. A public dataset goes in; four reviewed artifacts come out, and a person approves each stage before the next one starts. The intro video, infographic and these posts come out of the same workflow.

① Public dataset in: Ember yearly generation, 2000–2025
   ↓ profile + context
② Original analysis: strongest defensible story
   ↓ human review + approval
③ Pressure-tested deep-dive: checks claims, limits
   ↓ human review + approval
④ Dashboard: validated measures only
   ↓ human review + approval
⑤ Data story web app: "current" design language, live on GitHub
   ↓ final approval
⑥ /artifact + /post-social: video + infographic, every format
