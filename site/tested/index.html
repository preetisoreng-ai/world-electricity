How big a revision would undo the crossover?

Ember’s latest year is often revised. Slide to lower the 2025 renewables figure and raise coal by the same percentage. The lead disappears at 0.44%.

Take out hydro and renewables are far behind coal.

Hydropower is 13.9% of world electricity and swings with rainfall. Wind, solar and the rest add up to 19.6%, well below coal. That group has never been above coal in any year of the data.

We replayed 2025 with each year’s actual hydro swing from 2001 to 2025. With a year like 2001 (hydro −2.5%), renewables would not have passed coal. In 2025 hydro was −0.3%, close to normal.

Coal has fallen in a growing year before, and come back.

Coal generation fell while demand grew in 2015 and 2019, by far more than in 2025. Both times it later climbed to new records, reaching its highest ever in 2024. One year’s fall doesn’t make a peak.

What we still can’t answer.

Strongest supported conclusion. In Ember’s 2025 figures, clean sources grew by more than world demand, mostly thanks to solar, and renewables (led by hydro) edged past coal for the first time. Both are first-time events in this series, by small margins.

Not supported. That clean power is now replacing fossil fuels, that coal has peaked, or that wind and solar out-generate coal (they are 17.3% against 33.2%).

Is 2025 final? The file doesn’t say. Revision history isn’t in the data, so we show how big a revision would matter rather than how likely one is.

Hydro scenarios swap only the 2025 hydro figure, keeping every other source as reported. Real dry years also change gas and coal output, which this file can’t model.
