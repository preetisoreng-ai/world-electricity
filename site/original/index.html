Ember / World electricityOriginal analysis · 2000—2025

Renewables passed coal in 2025.

Renewable sources made 33.5% of the world’s electricity in 2025, coal 33.2%: the first time in 26 years of data. The same year, clean sources added 879 TWh, more than the 855 TWh rise in demand, and fossil generation slipped by 24 TWh.

What the data shows

Ember’s yearly totals of electricity generated worldwide, by source, from 2000 to 2025. Renewables here are hydro, wind, solar, bioenergy and other renewables.

What it doesn’t show

Countries, emissions, power-plant capacity, or why things changed. The newest year may still be revised.

01 / The crossover

After 25 years below coal, renewables crossed it.

Coal’s share peaked at 40.9% in 2007. Renewables were flat at about a fifth of generation until 2010, then climbed every year. In 2025 the lines crossed, by 0.3 percentage points.

To test A gap this small could close again if 2025 is revised, or if a dry year cuts hydro output. The next stage checks how fragile it is.

02 / Clean supply covers growth

Clean power covered all of 2025’s new demand.

Each year, the bars show how much clean and fossil generation changed; the dot is the change in total demand. In most years fossil fuels met much of the growth. In 2025 clean sources grew by more than total demand, so fossil generation fell slightly.

That happened before, in 2015 and 2019, but demand then grew by only 259 and 395 TWh. In 2025 it grew 855 TWh.

03 / Solar overtakes wind

Solar did most of the work, and passed wind.

Solar added 646 TWh in 2025, about three-quarters of the year’s growth in demand. At 2,807 TWh it now generates more than wind (2,705 TWh) for the first time. It is 11 times its 2015 level.

04 / The generation mix

The whole mix, 2000 against 2025.

Generation roughly doubled, from 15,325 to 31,925 TWh. Coal, nuclear, hydro and other fossil fuels lost share; gas gained almost four points; solar and wind went from almost nothing to about a sixth of the total between them.

05 / Method and limits

What this page can and can’t claim.

Source. Ember, yearly electricity generation, World, all sources, 2000–2025 (one CSV; no metadata or licence came with it). 260 rows: 26 years × 9 sources plus the total.

Checks. No duplicates or gaps. Sources add up to the total within 0.02 TWh each year, and the supplied year-on-year changes match a recompute within 0.01 TWh.

Groupings. Renewables = hydro, wind, solar, bioenergy, other renewables. Fossil = coal, gas, other fossil. Clean = renewables + nuclear. Shares use the supplied total.

Not yet tested: whether 2025 is final, how sensitive the 0.3-point crossover is to revisions or a weak hydro year, and whether “fossil fell” survives rounding (24 TWh is 0.1% of fossil output).

Out of scope: countries, emissions, capacity, prices and policy. The file can’t say why the mix changed.
