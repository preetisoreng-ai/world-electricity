Ember / World electricity2000—2025 / 26 annual observations

The current beneath the crossover

Renewables edged past coal in 2025. Total generation reached 31,925 TWh, 2.1× the 2000 level.

01 / The world’s electricity, in two currents

Each year has two bright endpoints: clean generation above the centreline, fossil below, on a shared scale. Clean = renewables + nuclear.

02 / Renewables pass coal

33.5% / 33.2%

33.5% and 33.2%: Renewables and coal, respectively, in 2025.

The first crossover in the 2000–2025 series, led by hydropower. Coal’s share peaked at 40.9% in 2007.

03 / Clean supply covers growth

+879 TWh

Plus 879 TWh. Clean generation rose 879 TWh against 855 TWh of total growth.

Fossil generation dipped 24 TWh (−0.1%). It also fell in 2015 and 2019, then rose to new records.

04 / Solar overtakes wind

2,807 TWh

2,807 TWh. Solar edged past wind’s 2,705 TWh.

It added 646 TWh in 2025, or 76% of total growth, and is 11× its 2015 level.

05 / The generation mix

Twenty-five years moved about a fifth of the world’s power.

    06 / The margin is narrow

    0.3 percentage points

    0.3 percentage points. A small lead in one year. Revisions or lower hydropower output could change the result.

    A 0.44% revision, or a dry year like 2001, would undo it. Without hydro, renewables are 19.6% of world power. The dataset covers generation; it gives no direct measure of emissions or the causes of the shift.
